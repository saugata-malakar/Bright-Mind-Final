=== UNDERSTANDING FRACTIONS AND ALGEBRA ===

1. What is a Fraction?
A fraction represents a part of a whole. It consists of a numerator (the top number, indicating how many parts we have) and a denominator (the bottom number, indicating how many equal parts the whole is divided into). For example, in 3/4, we have 3 parts of a whole that was split into 4 equal pieces.

2. Adding Fractions with Unlike Denominators
To add or subtract fractions that have different denominators, you must first find a common denominator.
- Step 1: Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of the denominators. This is the smallest number that both denominators can divide into evenly.
- Step 2: Convert each fraction so they both have this new denominator. To do this, multiply the numerator and the denominator of each fraction by the same number.
- Step 3: Add or subtract the numerators, keeping the denominator the same.
- Step 4: Simplify the fraction if possible.
Example: Adding 1/3 and 1/4. The LCM of 3 and 4 is 12. Convert 1/3 to 4/12, and 1/4 to 3/12. Now add: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.

3. Simplifying Fractions
To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and the denominator by their Greatest Common Divisor (GCD). For example, 6/8 can be simplified to 3/4 by dividing both 6 and 8 by 2.

4. Basic Algebra and Equations
An equation states that two expressions are equal. To solve for an unknown variable (like x), you must isolate the variable on one side of the equation.
Rule of Balances: Whatever operation you perform on one side of the equation, you must perform the exact same operation on the other side.
- To cancel addition, use subtraction.
- To cancel subtraction, use addition.
- To cancel multiplication, use division.
- To cancel division, use multiplication.
Example: For x + 5 = 12, subtract 5 from both sides to get x = 7.
